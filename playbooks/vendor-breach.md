---
title: Vendor breach
description: "A software provider, IT company, or other vendor that holds or reaches your data says it was breached: find your exposure, cut shared access, and know whose job notification is."
---

**Signs:** a notice from a vendor, news of a breach at a provider you use, or odd activity traced to an IT provider's remote tools.

## First moves

1. **Confirm the notice is real.** Breach news brings fake notices with phishing links. Go to the vendor's site or support line yourself.
2. **Ask the vendor in writing:** what happened and when, whether your data or accounts were involved, which data, what they've done, and who your contact is. Keep every reply.
3. **Rotate what you share with them:** passwords, API keys, tokens, and accounts they use to reach your systems.
4. **Limit their access** until they explain, if work can run without it. For an IT provider with remote access to your machines, consider disabling their tool until it's verified clean.

## Checks

- What does this vendor hold or reach? Use your system list from [get ready](../runbook/get-ready.md).
- Did the vendor's accounts or tools touch your systems during the breach window?
- Do your contracts or business associate agreements say who notifies whom, and by when?

## Who notifies

Usually the business with the relationship to the people affected gives the notice, and the vendor must tell that business. If you hold client data and your vendor lost it, notifying clients may be your job. If you hold data for a client, you tell the client. Settle it in writing with your lawyer, then work through [notify](../runbook/notify.md).

## Prevent the next one

Know which vendors hold what. Put breach notice deadlines, in hours or days, in contracts. Give vendors only the access they need, and review it yearly.

## Sources

- [New York General Business Law § 899-aa](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/899-AA), from the New York State Senate: a business holding data it doesn't own must notify the owner immediately, within 30 days.
- [45 CFR 164 Subpart D](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-D), HIPAA Breach Notification Rule: business associates notify the covered entity, which notifies individuals.
- [17 CFR 248.30](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section-248.30), Regulation S-P: service providers notify the covered institution within 72 hours.
- [16 CFR 314.4](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/section-314.4), FTC Safeguards Rule: overseeing service providers, at paragraph (f).
