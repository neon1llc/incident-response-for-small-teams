---
title: Vendor breach
description: "A software provider, IT company, or other vendor that holds or reaches your data says it was breached: find your exposure, cut shared access, and know whose job notification is."
---

**Signs:** a notice email from a vendor, news of a breach at a provider you use, or unusual activity traced back to an IT provider's remote tools.

## First moves

1. **Confirm the notice is real.** Breach news brings fake "breach notices" with phishing links. Go to the vendor's site or support line yourself.
2. **Ask the vendor, in writing:** what happened and when, whether your data or accounts were involved, which kinds of data, what they've done, and who your contact is. Keep every reply.
3. **Rotate what you share with them:** passwords, API keys, tokens, and any accounts they use to reach your systems.
4. **Limit their access** until they explain, if your work can run without it. For an IT provider with remote access to your machines, consider disabling their tool until it's verified clean.

## Checks

- What does this vendor hold or reach? Use your system list from [get ready](../runbook/get-ready.md).
- Is there activity in your own systems from the vendor's accounts or tools during the breach window?
- Do your contracts or business associate agreements say who notifies whom, and by when?

## Who notifies

Usually, the business that owns the relationship with the people affected gives the notice, and the vendor must tell that business. If you hold client data and your vendor lost it, notifying your clients may be your job, even though the breach was theirs. If you hold data for a client, you tell the client. Settle it in writing with your lawyer, and work through [notify](../runbook/notify.md).

## Prevent the next one

Know which vendors hold what. Put breach notice terms in contracts, with a deadline in hours or days. Give vendors only the access they need, and review it yearly.

## Sources

- [New York General Business Law § 899-aa](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/899-AA), from the New York State Senate: a business holding data it doesn't own must notify the owner immediately, within 30 days.
- [45 CFR 164 Subpart D](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-D), HIPAA Breach Notification Rule: business associates notify the covered entity, which notifies individuals.
- [17 CFR 248.30](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/section-248.30), Regulation S-P: service providers notify the covered institution within 72 hours.
- [16 CFR 314.4](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/section-314.4), FTC Safeguards Rule: overseeing service providers, at paragraph (f).
