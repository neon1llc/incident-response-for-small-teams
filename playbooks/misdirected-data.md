---
title: Misdirected data
description: "Client information was emailed, mailed, or shared with the wrong person, or a share link was open to anyone: get it back, confirm deletion, and decide whether it's a breach."
---

**Signs:** someone says "I think I sent that to the wrong client," a recipient replies "this isn't mine," an autocomplete picked the wrong name, or a folder link turns out to be open to anyone with it.

## First moves

1. **Stop further exposure:** recall the email if your system allows, expire or restrict the share link, and check the link's access log for who opened it.
2. **Contact the recipient** by phone if you can. Ask them to delete the information, including from their deleted items, and not to forward it. Ask them to confirm in writing.
3. **Write down exactly what was sent:** the file names, which people's information, and which kinds of data.
4. **Thank the person who reported it.** These are the incidents nobody hears about when staff are afraid to speak up.

## Notification questions

- **What was in it?** A name with a Social Security number, tax return, account number, or health information triggers most laws.
- **Who sent it, and who received it?** New York's law skips notice when someone authorized to access the data disclosed it by mistake and you reasonably decide misuse and harm are unlikely, for example when the recipient is another professional who deletes it and confirms. Write the decision down and keep it for five years. If more than 500 New York residents are involved, send that written determination to the Attorney General within 10 days. Other states handle mistaken disclosures differently.
- **For health information**, HIPAA has its own exceptions and a four-factor risk assessment, so record each factor.

Whatever you decide, record the decision and the reasons in the [notification worksheet](../templates/notification-worksheet.md), and see [notify](../runbook/notify.md).

## Prevent the next one

Turn off address autocomplete for outside recipients, or add a confirmation prompt for external mail with attachments. Send sensitive files through a client portal instead of email. Make share links expire, and restrict them to named people.

- [Breach Notification Rule](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/breach-notification/index.html), from HHS: HIPAA's exceptions and the four-factor risk assessment.
