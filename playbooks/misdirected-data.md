---
title: Misdirected data
description: "Client information went to the wrong person or an open share link: get it back and decide whether it's a breach."
---

## First moves

1. **Stop further exposure:** recall the email if you can, expire or restrict the share link, and check the link's access log for who opened it.
2. **Contact the recipient,** by phone if possible. Ask them to delete it, including from deleted items, not to forward it, and to confirm in writing.
3. **Write down exactly what was sent:** file names, whose information, and which kinds of data.
4. **Thank the person who reported it.**

## Notification questions

- **What was in it?** A name with a Social Security number, tax return, account number, or health information triggers most laws.
- **Who sent it and who received it?** New York's law skips notice when someone authorized to access the data disclosed it by mistake and you reasonably decide misuse and harm are unlikely, such as another professional who deletes it and confirms. Write the decision down and keep it for five years. If more than 500 New York residents are involved, send the written determination to the Attorney General within 10 days. Other states treat mistaken disclosures differently.
- **For health information,** HIPAA has its own exceptions and a four-factor risk assessment. Record each factor.

Record the decision and reasons in the [notification worksheet](../templates/notification-worksheet.md), and see [notify](../runbook/notify.md).
