---
title: Tabletop exercises
description: "Six ready-to-run tabletop exercises for small businesses, each 60 to 90 minutes, built around the incidents they see most."
---

A tabletop exercise is a structured "what would we do if...?" conversation around a table. Nobody touches a real system. The facilitator reveals a scenario in stages, and the team talks through what they'd know, do, and decide at each one. It's the cheapest way to learn that the insurer's number lives only in the mailbox you'd lose first.

Start with the [facilitator guide](facilitator-guide.md). It covers preparation, ground rules, timing, and the hotwash at the end.

## The exercises

| # | Exercise | Scenario | Good first pick for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [The wire that wasn't](01-the-wire-that-wasnt.md) | A supplier's "new bank details" email, and a payment that never arrives | Any business that pays invoices |
| 2 | [Monday morning ransom](02-monday-morning-ransom.md) | Files locked across the office, and a ransom note | Offices with a shared file server or synced folders |
| 3 | [Left in the cab](03-left-in-the-cab.md) | A laptop with client files disappears on the way home | Teams that work away from the office |
| 4 | [The forwarding rule](04-the-forwarding-rule.md) | A staff mailbox has been quietly forwarding mail for three weeks | Anyone on Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace |
| 5 | [Our vendor got hacked](05-our-vendor-got-hacked.md) | Your tax, practice management, or IT provider announces a breach | Businesses that rely on one key software vendor or MSP |
| 6 | [Reply all](06-reply-all.md) | A spreadsheet of client Social Security numbers goes to the wrong list | Every office that emails client files |

Each one matches a [playbook](../playbooks/index.md), so the team can check its answers against it afterward. For more scenarios, CISA's Tabletop Exercise Packages and the UK NCSC's Exercise in a Box are free.

## How each exercise is laid out

- **Setup:** who should attend, the objectives, and what to prepare.
- **Injects:** the scenario in three or four stages, each with the questions to ask.
- **What good looks like:** answers to listen for, linked to the runbook.
- **Curveballs:** optional twists for a team that's doing well.
- **Debrief:** questions for the hotwash, then fill in the [tabletop record](../templates/tabletop-record.md).

## How often

Once a year at minimum, and after a big change to staff, systems, or vendors. Rotate scenarios so each year tests something new, and rotate who plays incident lead, so the backup gets practice too.
