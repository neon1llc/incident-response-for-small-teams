title: Reply all
description: "A tabletop exercise on misdirected data: a spreadsheet of client Social Security numbers goes to the wrong mailing list, and the replies start coming in."
---

**Time:** 60 minutes. **Playbook:** [misdirected data](../playbooks/misdirected-data.md).

## Setup

**Attend:** the owner, the office manager, staff who send client documents, and IT or the MSP.

**Practice:** the first moves after a mistaken send (recall, contact, confirm), making sure the person who erred reports fast, and judging when a mistaken send is a reportable breach.

**Prepare:** nothing. It needs no technical knowledge, so it makes a good first exercise.

## Inject 1: Thursday, 3:45pm

An assistant meant to send your partner at another firm a spreadsheet of 60 clients' names, Social Security numbers, and filing statuses. Autocomplete picked the firm's newsletter list instead, with 1,200 subscribers. The assistant notices five minutes later.

**Ask:**
- What should the assistant do now? Would they feel safe telling someone?
- Does recall work for outside addresses? What do you send to the list?

## Inject 2: Thursday, 4:30pm

Fourteen subscribers have replied, mostly "I think this was sent to me by mistake." One forwarded it to their own accountant to ask if it's a scam. Another asks whether their own information is on it.

**Ask:**
- How do you ask 1,200 people to delete it, and confirm they did?
- What do you tell the person who asked if they're on it?
- Who decides whether this is a breach, and with whom?

## Inject 3: Friday

The lawyer asks how many of the 60 clients live in each state, which recipients opened the email, and whether anyone confirmed deletion.

**Ask:**
- Can you answer, and from where?
- Does New York's exception for an inadvertent disclosure by an authorized person fit?
- Who will you notify, and what will you say? Use the [notification worksheet](../templates/notification-worksheet.md).

## What good looks like

- The assistant reports within minutes and is thanked. See [is this an incident?](../runbook/is-this-an-incident.md)
- A short, plain message asks recipients to delete the email and not forward it, without repeating the contents.
- The exception depends on judging misuse unlikely, which a forward to an unknown accountant and 1,200 recipients make hard to claim. Whatever is decided is written down with reasons.

## Debrief

1. Would the person who erred have told us within five minutes? How do we know?
2. Should SSNs go by email at all? Can we warn on external attachments?

Record it on the [tabletop record](../templates/tabletop-record.md).
