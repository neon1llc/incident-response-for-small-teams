---
title: Reply all
description: "A tabletop exercise on misdirected data: a spreadsheet of client Social Security numbers goes to the wrong mailing list, and the replies start coming in."
---

**Time:** 60 minutes. **Playbook:** [misdirected data](../playbooks/misdirected-data.md).

## Setup

**Attend:** the owner, the office manager, staff who send client documents, and IT or the MSP.

**Objectives:**

1. Practice the first moves after a mistaken send: recall, contact, confirm.
2. Make sure the person who made the mistake would report it quickly.
3. Work through when a mistaken send is, and isn't, a reportable breach.

**Prepare:** nothing beyond the usual. This one works well as a first exercise because nobody needs technical knowledge.

## Inject 1: Thursday, 3:45pm

An assistant meant to send a spreadsheet of 60 clients' names, Social Security numbers, and filing statuses to your partner at another firm. Autocomplete picked the firm's newsletter list instead: 1,200 subscribers. The assistant notices the mistake five minutes later.

**Ask:**
- What should the assistant do in the next five minutes? Would they feel safe telling someone?
- Can the email be recalled? Does recall work for outside addresses?
- What do you send to the list, if anything?

## Inject 2: Thursday, 4:30pm

Fourteen subscribers have replied. Most say "I think this was sent to me by mistake." One forwarded it to their own accountant to ask whether it's a scam. Another asks whether their own information is on it.

**Ask:**
- How do you ask 1,200 people to delete it, and how do you confirm they did?
- What do you say to the person who asked whether they're on it?
- Who decides whether this is a breach, and with whom?

## Inject 3: Friday

The lawyer asks: how many of the 60 clients live in each state, which of the 1,200 recipients opened the email, and whether anyone confirmed deletion.

**Ask:**
- Can you answer those questions? From where?
- Does New York's exception for an inadvertent disclosure by an authorized person fit? Why or why not?
- Who will you notify, and what will you say? Use the [notification worksheet](../templates/notification-worksheet.md).

## What good looks like

- The assistant reports within minutes, and is thanked for it. See [is this an incident?](../runbook/is-this-an-incident.md)
- A short, plain message asks recipients to delete the email and not forward it, without repeating the contents.
- The team sees that the exception for mistaken disclosures depends on judging misuse unlikely, and that a forward to an unknown accountant and 1,200 recipients make that hard to claim. Whatever is decided is written down with reasons.

## Curveballs

- The spreadsheet also included two clients' bank account numbers for direct deposit.
- A local reporter who subscribes to the newsletter calls.

## Debrief

1. Would the person who made the mistake have told us within five minutes? How do we know?
2. Should SSNs go by email at all? What would replace it?
3. Can we turn off autocomplete for outside addresses, or add a warning for external attachments?

Record it on the [tabletop record](../templates/tabletop-record.md).
