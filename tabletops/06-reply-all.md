title: Reply all
description: "A tabletop exercise on misdirected data: client Social Security numbers go to the wrong mailing list."
---

**Time:** 60 minutes. **Playbook:** [misdirected data](../playbooks/misdirected-data.md). **Attend:** the owner, the office manager, staff who send client documents, and IT or the MSP. **Prepare:** nothing. It needs no technical knowledge, so it makes a good first exercise.

## Inject 1: Thursday, 3:45pm

An assistant meant to send a partner at another firm a spreadsheet of 60 clients' names and Social Security numbers. Autocomplete picked the firm's newsletter list instead, with 1,200 subscribers. The assistant notices five minutes later.

**Ask:** What should the assistant do now? Would they feel safe telling someone?

## Inject 2: Thursday, 4:30pm

Fourteen subscribers have replied, mostly "I think this was sent to me by mistake." One forwarded it to their own accountant to ask if it's a scam. Another asks whether their own information is on it.

**Ask:** How do you ask 1,200 people to delete it, and confirm they did? Who decides whether this is a breach?

## Inject 3: Friday

The lawyer asks how many of the 60 clients live in each state, which recipients opened the email, and whether anyone confirmed deletion.

**Ask:** Can you answer, and from where? Does New York's exception for an inadvertent disclosure by an authorized person fit? Use the [notification worksheet](../templates/notification-worksheet.md).

## What good looks like

- The assistant reports within minutes and is thanked. See [is this an incident?](../runbook/is-this-an-incident.md)
- A short, plain message asks recipients to delete the email and not forward it, without repeating the contents.
- The exception depends on judging misuse unlikely, which a forward to an unknown accountant and 1,200 recipients make hard to claim. Whatever is decided is written down with reasons.

**Debrief:** Would the person who erred have told us within five minutes?
