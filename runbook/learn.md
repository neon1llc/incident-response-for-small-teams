Hold the after-action review within two weeks of closing, for every level 1 or 2 incident and any level 3 that surprised you.

## Run the review

**Who:** everyone with a role, plus the person who first reported it. **Time:** 45 to 60 minutes. **Rule:** blameless. Ask what made the mistake easy, not who made it. People stop reporting when reporting gets them blamed.

Walk the [incident log](../templates/incident-log.md) in order, then ask:

1. What happened, and when did we first know? How long was the gap?
2. What went well?
3. Where did we lose time, and why?
4. What did we need and not have: a number, a password, a log, a decision?
5. What made it possible, and what would have caught it sooner? Expect several factors, such as a missing control, an unclear owner, and a confusing tool, not one root cause.
6. What will we change, who owns each change, and by when?

## Turn findings into fixes

- Keep the list short. Three fixes that get done beat ten that don't.
- Give every fix an owner and a date, and check them at the next staff meeting.
- Update the [response plan](../templates/response-plan.md), [contact sheet](../templates/contact-sheet.md), and any [playbook](../playbooks/index.md) that was wrong or missing.

## Keep the record

File the [after-action report](../templates/after-action-report.md) with the incident log, notification worksheet, and copies of every notice sent. Insurers, auditors, and regulators may ask for them. The FTC Safeguards Rule also requires covered businesses holding information on 5,000 or more consumers to evaluate and revise their incident response plan after a security event.
