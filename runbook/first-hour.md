title: The first hour
description: "What to do in the first 60 minutes after someone reports something wrong."
---

Print this page.

## Minutes 0 to 10: take the report

1. **Write down what the reporter saw,** when, and on which device or account. Ask them to touch nothing else.
2. **Ask the four questions** from [is this an incident?](is-this-an-incident.md): is money moving, could someone be in our systems now, could personal data be involved, can we still work?
3. **Set a severity level.** When unsure, pick the higher one.

## Minutes 10 to 20: declare and assign

4. **Declare it by call or text on your backup channel,** not email: "Level 2 incident. I'm leading. Dana is scribe, Sam is tech lead. Next check-in 10:30." Use a plain two-word codename, such as "Blue Kettle," so messages don't say "breach" before anyone knows.
5. **Name the tech lead, scribe, and communications.** The scribe opens the [incident log](../templates/incident-log.md) and backfills.
6. **Check in about every 30 minutes.** Between check-ins, let people work. Raise disagreements at the [review](learn.md).

## Minutes 20 to 40: stop the damage

7. **If money moved, call the bank first.** Ask its fraud department to recall or freeze the transfer, and report it at **ic3.gov**. Fast reports give wire transfers the best chance of coming back.
8. **Contain what you can see,** following [contain](contain.md). Reset a compromised account's password from a clean device and sign it out everywhere. Unplug an infected computer's network cable, turn off Wi-Fi, and **leave it powered on,** because memory holds evidence. Lock or wipe a lost device.
9. **Don't delete, reimage, pay, or contact the attacker.** Each destroys evidence or options.

## Minutes 40 to 60: call for help

10. **Call your IT provider's** after-hours line.
11. **For level 1 or 2, call your cyber insurer's** claim line. Many policies require notice within a set time and assign the breach coach, lawyer, and forensics firm. Ask before hiring anyone yourself.
12. **Call a lawyer** if personal information may be involved, because notification clocks may already be running. See [notify](notify.md).
13. **Tell staff only what they need:** what to avoid, how to reach the lead, and not to discuss it outside the company.

At one hour, the lead logs what's known, what's contained, who's been called, and the severity now. Then open [contain](contain.md), [investigate](investigate.md), and the matching [playbook](../playbooks/index.md).

## Sources

- [Ransomware Guide](https://www.cisa.gov/stopransomware/ransomware-guide), from CISA: isolate affected systems, keep them powered on, and use out-of-band communication.
- [Business email compromise](https://www.fbi.gov/how-we-can-help-you/scams-and-safety/common-frauds-and-scams/business-email-compromise), from the FBI: call the bank and report to IC3 at once after a fraudulent transfer.
- [Internet Crime Complaint Center](https://www.ic3.gov/), from the FBI: where to report, and why speed matters for recovering funds.
- [Data Breach Response: A Guide for Business](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/data-breach-response-guide-business), from the FTC: secure operations, assemble a team, and preserve evidence.
