---
title: Get ready
description: "What to do before an incident: a plan, a contact sheet, logs, backups, insurance, and a yearly drill."
---

Most of what goes wrong was decided months earlier: nobody knew who could shut the office down, the insurer's number was in the hacked mailbox, or the backups had never been restored.

- [ ] **Write the plan.** Fill in the one-page [response plan](../templates/response-plan.md) and have the owner sign it.
- [ ] **Fill in the [contact sheet](../templates/contact-sheet.md).** Print it. Keep one copy in the office and one at the lead's home.
- [ ] **Agree on a backup channel** for when email and chat are compromised, such as a group text or call bridge.
- [ ] **List your systems,** who administers each, and where personal information lives. You can't decide who to notify without it.
- [ ] **Turn on sign-in and audit logs** for email and cloud accounts and keep them at least 90 days. Many small-business plans keep very little by default.
- [ ] **Test a restore.** Keep one backup copy offline or immutable, and restore a real file every quarter.
- [ ] **Read your insurance policy.** Find out whether you have cyber coverage, how fast you must report, and whether you must use the insurer's lawyers and forensics firms. Using your own can cost you coverage.
- [ ] **Get your IT provider's incident terms in writing:** hours, rates, and limits.
- [ ] **Tell staff how to report:** one name, one number, and "tell us, don't fix it."
- [ ] **Run a [tabletop exercise](../tabletops/index.md)** yearly.

The FTC Safeguards Rule requires a written incident response plan for tax preparers, mortgage brokers, some car dealers, and other non-bank financial businesses, unless you hold information on fewer than 5,000 consumers. The HIPAA Security Rule requires one for health practices and their business associates. Insurers ask about it, and SOC 2 auditors test it.

## Sources

- [NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/61/r3/final), from NIST: preparation as part of Govern, Identify, and Protect.
- [Small Business Guide: Response and Recovery](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/small-business-guidance--response-and-recovery/step-1-prepare-for-incidents), from the UK National Cyber Security Centre: cover for absence, decision trigger points, and keeping contacts reachable.
- [Incident Response Plan Basics](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/publications/Incident-Response-Plan-Basics_508c.pdf), from CISA: plan elements, roles, and a backup communication channel.
- [16 CFR 314.4](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/section-314.4), FTC Safeguards Rule: the required written incident response plan, at paragraph (h).
- [16 CFR 314.6](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/section-314.6), FTC Safeguards Rule: the written plan requirement doesn't apply to institutions holding information on fewer than 5,000 consumers.
- [45 CFR 164.308](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/section-164.308), HIPAA Security Rule: security incident procedures and contingency planning.
- [Ransomware Guide](https://www.cisa.gov/stopransomware/ransomware-guide), from CISA: offline backups, restore testing, and log retention.
- [Safeguarding Taxpayer Data](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p4557.pdf), IRS Publication 4557: security and incident planning for tax professionals.
