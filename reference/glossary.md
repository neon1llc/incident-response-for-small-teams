---
title: Glossary
description: "Plain definitions of the incident response terms used in this kit, from after-action review to tabletop exercise."
---

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **After-action review** | A blameless meeting after an incident or exercise to record what worked, what didn't, and what will change. Also called a hotwash right after an exercise. See [learn](../runbook/learn.md) |
| **Breach** | An incident in which personal information was, or may have been, seen or taken by someone unauthorized. Each notification law has its own exact definition. See [is this an incident?](../runbook/is-this-an-incident.md) |
| **Breach coach** | A lawyer, usually assigned by a cyber insurer, who coordinates the response and decides on notification |
| **Business email compromise (BEC)** | Fraud using a hacked or look-alike email account to redirect payments or steal data. See the [playbook](../playbooks/business-email-compromise.md) |
| **Chain of custody** | A record of who collected each piece of evidence, when, and where it's been kept since |
| **Containment** | Steps that cut off an attacker's access or stop harm from spreading. See [contain](../runbook/contain.md) |
| **Discovery** | The moment the business knew, or should have known, about an incident. Most notification deadlines count from here |
| **Event** | Anything unusual someone noticed. Most events aren't incidents, but all should be reported |
| **Forensics** | Investigation of devices, accounts, and logs to establish what happened, usually by a specialist firm |
| **Incident** | An event that threatens, or has harmed, systems, data, or money |
| **Incident lead** | The person who declares an incident, sets priorities, and makes decisions during it |
| **Indicator of compromise (IOC)** | A trace an attacker leaves, such as a sign-in address, file name, or forwarding rule, used to search for other affected systems |
| **Inject** | A new piece of information a facilitator adds during a tabletop exercise, such as "a client calls about a strange email" |
| **Managed service provider (MSP)** | An outside company that runs a business's IT |
| **Multifactor authentication (MFA)** | Signing in with something beyond a password, such as an app prompt, a code, or a security key. Also called two-step sign-in |
| **Ransomware** | Malware that locks files and demands payment, often after copying data out first. See the [playbook](../playbooks/ransomware.md) |
| **Severity level** | How serious an incident is, from 1 (critical) to 4 (low), which sets how fast you move and who gets called |
| **Tabletop exercise** | A discussion-based drill where a team talks through a realistic scenario without touching real systems. See [tabletop exercises](../tabletops/index.md) |
